## 3. Proposal for Stage H2 Step 1: Feasibility and Diagnostic Studies

To investigate the distance toward the target goal of **0.80 OOF ROC-AUC** and understand dataset constraints, we propose three diagnostic analyses:

### Component A: Learning Curves (Data Scalability)
- **Objective**: Determine whether the models are currently data-starved or sample-saturated.
- **Methodology**: Train LightGBM across the 5 stratified folds using training fractions of **25%** (~30,000 samples), **50%** (~60,000 samples), **75%** (~90,000 samples), and **100%** (~120,000 samples per fold).
- **Metric**: Record validation ROC-AUC at each fraction and compute the rate of marginal gain per additional 25% data.

### Component B: Feature-Importance Concentration
- **Objective**: Measure how heavily the models depend on a small subset of features (specifically `EXT_SOURCE_1/2/3`) versus utilizing broader applicant features.
- **Methodology**: Aggregate split-count and gain-based feature importances across all 5 folds to identify dominant predictors and discover uninformative/redundant features.

### Component C: Adversarial Validation (Train vs. Test Distribution Shift)
- **Objective**: Determine whether `train.csv` and `test.csv` come from identical statistical distributions or exhibit temporal/covariate drift.
- **Methodology**: Create a binary classification dataset combining train (`label = 0`, 171,202 rows) and test (`label = 1`, 61,500 rows). Train a LightGBM model to predict whether a sample belongs to test.
  - An adversarial ROC-AUC $\approx 0.50$ confirms identical distributions (ideal).
  - An adversarial ROC-AUC $\ge 0.70$ reveals significant distribution shift, pinpointing the drifting features.

### Component D: Reference Estimate of Gap to 0.80 ROC-AUC
- **Current Best Ensemble**: `0.76203`
- **Target Reference**: `0.80000`
- **Remaining Gap**: `+0.03797` ROC-AUC.
- **Contextual Interpretation**:
  - This gap is presented solely as an empirical benchmark, **not as a theoretical upper bound**.
  - Neither learning curves nor adversarial validation alone will definitively prove that 0.80 is impossible. Rather, they diagnose what type of modeling (e.g. higher-order interaction features, external source imputation, or target encoding) holds the highest leverage.
